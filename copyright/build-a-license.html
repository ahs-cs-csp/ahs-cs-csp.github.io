Build a License

Part 3 of 6 · Unit 2 Lab 5. Copyright is automatic and it says no by default. Creative Commons exists so you can say yes on your own terms — here is where it came from, and how to build one.

How we got here

  1. 1998

    Congress extends copyright by 20 years

    The Sonny Bono Copyright Term Extension Act pushes terms to life of the author plus 70 years. For the next two decades, essentially nothing new enters the public domain.

  2. 1999

    Eric Eldred sues

    Eldred put public-domain books online for free. The extension froze his supply of new material. A law professor named Lawrence Lessig takes the case.

  3. 2001

    Creative Commons is founded

    Lessig, Hal Abelson and Eldred start it while the case is still running. Abelson co-wrote Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs — the book Berkeley's Brian Harvey taught from, and BJC, this course, descends from that line.

  4. Dec 2002

    The first CC licenses are published

    Not a change to the law. A set of ready-made permissions an author can attach to their own work, written so a human, a lawyer and a computer can all read them.

  5. Jan 2003

    Eldred loses, 7–2

    The Supreme Court says Congress may set terms however it likes, so long as they are not literally forever. Justices Stevens and Breyer dissent. The extension stands.

  6. Jan 2003

    Cory Doctorow gives his novel away on the day it goes on sale

    Down and Out in the Magic Kingdom is published and simultaneously released as a free CC download — the first novel to do it. It sold well. His argument: for a new writer the problem is not piracy, it is obscurity.

  7. 2009

    Wikipedia relicenses to CC BY-SA

    The largest reference work ever made runs on one of these licenses.

  8. Today

    BJC is CC BY-NC-SA

    The curriculum open in your other tab right now. Someone chose those letters on purpose. By the end of this page you will know exactly what they chose.

The part worth noticing: they lost. The law did not change. So instead of fighting it, they built a tool that works inside copyright — using an author's own exclusive rights to hand permission away in advance.

Build one

You wrote something. Decide what other people may do with it.

BY cannot be switched off. Every Creative Commons license requires credit — that is the one thing you never give up.

Pick the license

Six combinations are legal. Which fits?

Three things people mix up

Free software

About liberty. You may run, read, change and pass on the code. The Free Software Foundation's position is ethical: users deserve this control.

Open source

Same licenses, different pitch. Framed as better engineering — more eyes, faster fixes, lower cost. Coined in 1998 to sell the idea to companies.

Open access

Not software at all. Research papers readable without a subscription — the work is usually publicly funded, so the public can read it.

“Free” here means free as in speech, not free as in pizza. Free software can be sold; open-source code can sit inside a product you pay for.
